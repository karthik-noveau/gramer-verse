The mark

A ball inside a box — the preposition in, which is the idea every other lesson is built on. The gap in the top edge is the ball's own width, so it reads as the way in rather than as a missing line. The frame is ink rather than accent so the mark survives on a green ground; the ball takes the accent, whose one job in this system is to mark the thing that moved.

The bilingual logo

The product is English taught in Tamil, so a logo in one script tells half the story. It is the wordmark that carries both, not the mark: Latin leads because the subject is English, and sits under it, smaller and muted, because it is the pronunciation rather than a second name — the same transliteration bridge the lessons use. Both wordmarks are outlines, not live text: the prototype ships no Tamil webfont, and a wordmark that can render as an empty box is not a wordmark.

Why not a bilingual mark

Putting both alphabets inside the mark was tried first. Tamil letterforms carry more detail than a circle does, and the mark has to hold at 16px — every version below is legible at 48 and a smudge at 16.
